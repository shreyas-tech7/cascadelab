Decision guide

  • Start from strong only (94.4% held-out accuracy, $0.0662 per 1,000 tasks). Every other strategy is judged by the paired difference against it.
  • A cascade on a visible check is the first thing to try when your task has a check: it kept 87.5% of tasks on the cheap model and cost $0.0148 per 1,000 tasks. Its accuracy is 84.7%, because it can only catch failures that the check can see.
  • A cascade on agreement needs no checker, but it pays for 5 cheap samples on every task. Here it cost $0.0738 per 1,000 tasks, against $0.0662 for strong only, and escalated 68.1% of tasks. One cheap call costs about 8.9% of one strong call here, so 5 cheap samples cost 44.6% of a strong call. It saves money only when it escalates fewer than 55.4% of tasks.
  • The learned router escalated 33.3% of tasks before any cheap call and reached 79.2%. It needs labeled development data, and it overfits: 90.7% on its own training split.
  • The oracle router (98.6% for $0.0218) is the ceiling for any router with these two models. Nobody can build it. Use it to see how much room is left.
  • If an interval on the paired difference crosses zero, you have not shown a difference. Get more tasks before you ship.
